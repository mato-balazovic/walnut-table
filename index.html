The walnut table

I'm making a round dining table from two slabs of the same walnut tree. A black resin river joins them. Four curved legs carry the top.

Top
110 cm round, 4 cm thick
Height
79 cm
Wood

01The wood

It started with two slabs

They are a brother and a sister, cut from the same walnut tree.

That's why their colour and grain match. They still have their natural, wavy edges.

Each slab has one flaw I decided to keep. One has a big knot. The other has a hole that goes right through. Both get filled with black resin and stay on show.

What oil does

Raw walnut looks dusty and grey. Oil brings out the dark chocolate colour and the pattern of the grain.

Drag across the picture to compare the slabs as they are today with the same wood oiled.

02The top

Two slabs, one river

The two wavy edges face each other across the middle of the table. The gap between them becomes a river of black resin.

The river is 1.5 cm wide at its narrowest and about 9.5 cm at its widest. The knot and the hole get the same black fill.

On the real wood

This is the same top, laid out from photos of the two pieces I cut. The river follows their natural edges.

Six ways to place the slabs

Before cutting anything, I tried six layouts as mock-ups from my photos. The glued ones join the two edges directly, with no river. I chose Diagonal.

03The legs

Four legs, one line each

The base is four sculpted walnut legs that meet in the middle. Each one reaches out under the top, pinches in at the waist and splays out to the floor.

The idea comes from the Spider Table by Foureyes Furniture, which stands on six legs. Mine is adapted to four legs and a 110 cm top.

The legs will come from the offcuts of the two slabs, plus some extra walnut.

05The miniature

The table, ten times smaller

On the weekend of 7 and 8 November I made a miniature of the table.

Everything is ten times smaller. The top is 11 cm across and it stands just under 8 cm tall.

06Build diary

From slabs to table

  1. Up next

    The circle and the pour

    Next I lay the two pieces out, set the river and mark the 110 cm circle. Then a mould goes around them and the black resin goes in. The circle gets cut after the pour.

    The legs come after the top.

  2. 30 Oct 2026Latest

    Bark off, holes cleaned

    I took the bark and the soft layer off the two facing edges, back to firm wood. The wavy shape stays.

    Then I picked the knot and the hole clean. Both go right through, so the mould has to seal them from below.

  3. 25 Oct 2026

    First cuts

    I cut both slabs to 127 cm, a little longer than the top, so there is spare at the ends. The offcuts are set aside for the legs.

  4. Aug to Oct 2026

    Designing on the computer

    Before any cutting, I measured and photographed both slabs and modelled the whole table on the computer.
